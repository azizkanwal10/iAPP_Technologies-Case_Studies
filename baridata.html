The brief

Baridata replaces spreadsheets with one central registry for bariatric surgery. Surgeons capture each case quickly, clinical staff manage follow-ups, dashboards update as data comes in, and anonymised datasets are ready for audits and research.

Bariatric programmes depend on long-term data: weight loss, diabetes remission, nutrition and complications, tracked for years after surgery. That data sat across spreadsheets and separate systems, follow-up visits were missed, and preparing a dataset for a study or an audit took a great deal of manual cleaning. The goal was a single platform that makes capture fast and keeps outcomes visible every day.

Who it serves: Bariatric surgeons, clinical staff and hospital programmes across India that report on outcomes and publish research.

How it works

What made it hard

Documentation eats surgeon time

Procedures were being recorded across multiple systems and spreadsheets, and every centre recorded them differently.

Follow-ups slip

Missed visits leave gaps in long-term outcome data, which is exactly the data a bariatric programme is judged on.

Outcomes arrive weeks late

Without live dashboards, surgeons waited weeks for reports and summaries.

Research and benchmarking are slow

Datasets needed heavy cleaning before analysis, and hospitals had no easy way to compare their results with peers.

What we built

Digital surgery registry

Patient demographics, procedure details and early outcomes captured in about two minutes.

Follow-up management

Scheduled visits with reminders and alerts for missed appointments.

Live outcomes dashboard

%TWL, %EWL, diabetes remission and complication rates, updated as data is entered.

Per-patient timeline

Weight-loss progress, nutritional markers, HbA1c trends and long-term recovery for each patient.

National benchmarking

Compare surgeons and hospitals against bariatric procedures nationwide.

Research-ready export

Anonymised CSV and Excel datasets for audits, clinical studies and publications.

How we worked

  1. Design for the operating day

    Capture was built to take about two minutes, so surgeons record every case rather than catching up later.

  2. Make follow-ups automatic

    Reminders and missing-visit alerts replace manual tracking.

  3. Turn records into live metrics

    Dashboards and patient timelines update straight from the registry, with no separate reporting step.

  4. Build for research

    Anonymised exports and benchmarking make the same data useful for studies, audits and quality improvement.

Where it landed

One registry for the whole bariatric programme: surgeries logged in minutes, follow-ups tracked automatically, outcomes visible live and research data ready when it is needed.
