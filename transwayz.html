The brief

Transwayz brings orders, dispatch, tracking, invoicing, driver settlements and fuel-tax reporting into one online system. Many trucking companies run on it at the same time, and each one only ever sees its own data.

Trucking companies were running their business across phone calls, spreadsheets, accounting tools and separate driving-hours logs. The goal was a single online system that follows a load end to end: a customer asks for a move, the team creates an order, a dispatcher assigns a driver, truck and trailer, the trip is tracked to delivery, and then the customer is invoiced and the driver and partners are paid.

Who it serves: Built for small and mid-sized carriers and the dispatchers, drivers and accounts teams who work inside them.

What made it hard

Many companies, one system

Every carrier shares the same platform, so data separation had to be built into the foundation: each company sees only its own customers, trips and money.

Data from the truck itself

Location and legal driving hours live in electronic logging devices from different vendors. Dispatch needed that data automatically, not re-typed.

Money in, money out

Customer invoices, driver pay, carrier pay, vendor bills and bank matching all had to close the loop on the same trip record.

Compliance paperwork

Fuel tax (IFTA) reporting needs miles by state or province, pulled from real trip and fuel data rather than estimates.

What we built

Orders

Multi-stop pickups and deliveries, copy and reverse for return trips, recurring daily or weekly orders, drafts, and bulk import from spreadsheets.

Trips and dispatch

Assign a driver, truck and trailer or an outside carrier. Every stage is time-stamped, from planned to completed, with documents, photos and a printable trip sheet.

Live map and driving hours

Trucks on a live map, with each driver's remaining legal hours visible before a load is assigned.

ELD connections

Location and hours pulled in automatically from Samsara, Motive, Geotab, Omnitracs, PeopleNet, Isaac and Fleet Hunt.

Accounting

Invoices emailed as PDFs, driver settlements with bonuses and deductions, carrier pay, vendor bills, automatic paid status and bank reconciliation.

IFTA and reporting

Fuel-tax and mileage reports by state, plus sales, trip profitability, fuel economy and unpaid-invoice ageing with spreadsheet and PDF export.

Access and audit

Admin approval for new users, five role-based views, and an activity log that records who changed what and when.

Marketing site and blog

A public website and blog so the Transwayz team can attract and convert new carriers.

How we worked

  1. Map the load

    We modelled the real business flow first: order, trip, delivery, invoice, settlement, report. Every screen hangs off that chain.

  2. Design by role

    Admin, Dispatcher, Driver, Accounts and Reports each get only the screens they need, which keeps a dense product calm to use.

  3. Integrate the fleet

    ELD providers were connected one by one so trip status and driving hours flow in without manual entry.

  4. Close the books

    Accounting, reconciliation and IFTA were built on the same trip data, so operations and finance never disagree.

Where it landed

A single place where a carrier can take an order, dispatch it, watch it move, bill for it and pay everyone involved, with reports that show how the business is doing.
